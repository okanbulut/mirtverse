Item Response Theory with mirt and mirtverse

A Datasets used in the book

Every example loads its data in one chunk, labelled data-<chapter>-<n>, at the start of the example. The table lists what each chunk defines. Real data come with mirt; simulated data are generated in the chunk itself, so the true parameters are known and the examples can check estimates against them.

The book deliberately does not analyse the mirtverse lab datasets (certexam, numeracy, linking, catbank and openended), because the labs ask students to discover problems hidden in them. The same goes for the AI Hub datasets itemtext and essays. They are described in the teaching labs article and on their help pages, for example ?certexam.

Other datasets for practice

mirt ships more datasets that work well for exercises:

  • LSAT6 and LSAT7: Short dichotomous tests (expand them with expand.table()).
  • deAyala: Five dichotomous mathematics items from de Ayala’s textbook, as a frequency table.
  • SLF: Five dichotomous statements about social life feelings, answered by 1,490 respondents.
  • Bock1997: A three-item polytomous test, as a frequency table.
  • ASVAB: Four arithmetic reasoning items with response counts for four groups defined by sex and race, a ready-made exercise for multiple-group models and DIF.

Use ?<name> for the details of each.
